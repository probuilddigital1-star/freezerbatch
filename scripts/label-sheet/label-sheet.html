Freezer Batch Cocktails · free printable

Before you print

This sheet is free — print as many copies as you need. One minute on paper stock and timing is the difference between a label that survives the freezer and one that curls off in a week.

01

Skip die-cut label sheets

This grid doesn’t match any pre-cut template, so die-cut sheets go to waste — and paper label stock fails in a freezer anyway. Moisture wicks in at every cut edge, travels under the face, and separates it from the adhesive. A cut-your-own label has four cut edges: the worst case for exactly that failure.

02

Print on full-sheet label stock or plain cardstock

Full-sheet (8.5×11) adhesive label stock, or ordinary cardstock. Print this sheet, then cut along the dashed lines.

03

Apply warm and dry — before the first freeze

Adhesives have a minimum application temperature well above your freezer. A label pressed onto a cold or frosted bottle never bonds. Fill in the label, stick it to a dry, room-temperature bottle, and then batch and freeze.

04

Or skip adhesive entirely

Cut the label from cardstock, punch a hole in the corner, and hang it off the bottle neck with twine or a rubber band. Nothing to peel, nothing to fail, and it moves to the next batch with the bottle.
