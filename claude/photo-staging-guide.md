## The honesty rule, sharpened

Rev. 2's rule stands: staging the liquid in a hero shot is ordinary food photography, and
staged shots never carry tested or tasted claims. Observed-storage data, test dates and
process imagery come from real batches.

Rev. 3 adds the line that matters for substitutions:

> A stand-in is fine when the reader's drink will look the same. It is not fine when the
> stand-in changes the colour, clarity or texture they will actually get.

Vodka standing in for white rum in a Daiquiri is invisible, because both are clear and
the lime does all the visible work. Campari standing in for Aperol is not, because
Campari is deep ruby at 25% and Aperol is bright orange at 11%. The photo is a promise
about what comes out of the reader's bottle. Keep the promise.

## What the camera sees

Colour, clarity, garnish, vessel. Not flavour, proof, or brand.

- Clear spirits photograph as water: vodka, gin, white rum, blanco tequila.
- Rye and bourbon are indistinguishable on camera. One owned whiskey covers every whiskey
  drink.
- Citrus drinks need real juice. Lemon or lime plus water plus a little 1:1 syrup, shaken,
  gives the authentic haze and fine bubbles that nothing else fakes.
- Espresso martini needs no liquor at all. Real coffee plus syrup, shaken hard, makes the
  identical crema crown.
- Garnish is always real.

## Session 1: free, tonight, nothing bought

Two recipes, both fully covered by what is already in the house. One of them is the
number two traffic page.

**Vesper.** Spec is gin 60, vodka 15, Cocchi Americano 22, at 32% ABV. On camera it is a
near-clear pale straw. Pour vodka with a splash of water into a chilled martini glass and
express a lemon peel over it, which is exactly what the page says to do. Visually
identical to the real thing. Eight pageviews and no photo.

**Daiquiri.** White rum 60, lime 22, simple syrup 15. Vodka instead of the rum, real lime
juice, real simple syrup made from sugar and water. Shake it so it clouds properly, pour
into a chilled coupe, lime wheel. The lime haze is the whole look and it will be real.

Both are served up, so this is a coupe and martini glass session. **Prop check: confirm
you own a martini glass.** The dirty martini was shot in a stemmed bowl; if that was a
coupe, the Vesper and Cosmopolitan both need a proper martini glass or they should be
restaged in the coupe with the page text updated to match.

## Session 2: one grocery run, roughly $10

**Cosmopolitan.** Vodka 45, Cointreau 15, cranberry 30, lime 15. The cranberry is the
entire colour and it costs about four dollars. Cointreau is clear and contributes nothing
visible, so skip it for the shot. Martini glass, flamed orange peel, which means buying an
orange. Six pageviews.

**Moscow Mule.** Vodka 60, lime 15, ginger syrup 15, topped with ginger beer, served in a
copper mug. The mug is the photograph. Almost no liquid is visible, so this shot is about
the copper, the condensation and the lime wheel. **Prop check: do you own a copper mug?**
If not, this is blocked on a prop rather than an ingredient, and at roughly zero
pageviews it is not worth buying one for. Defer it if the mug is not already in the house.

**Espresso Martini.** Vodka 45, Kahlua 30, cold brew concentrate 30. Strong cold coffee
plus a little simple syrup gets the colour without buying Kahlua. Buy whole coffee beans
for the three-bean garnish.

That one is a technique shot, not an ingredient shot. The crema crown is the entire
photograph and it starts collapsing within about a minute. Shoot it first in its session,
camera already framed and focused on an empty glass, then shake hard, pour, and fire
immediately. Expect to make it twice.

## Session 3: one bottle, sweet vermouth, roughly $12

**Boulevardier.** Bourbon 45, Campari 30, sweet vermouth 30. You own the bourbon and the
Campari. Sweet vermouth is the only gap, and it is the bottle that buys the most, because
it is also an ingredient in Hanky-Panky and Bijou. Rocks glass, orange peel
expressed and dropped in.

This one gets made for real, all three ingredients, no substitution.

## The one spend worth arguing about: Paper Plane

Equal parts bourbon 22, Aperol 22, Amaro Nonino 22, lemon 22. The page specifies no
garnish at all. That means the colour of the liquid is one hundred percent of the
photograph, with nothing else to look at.

Your Campari substitution will not work here, and this is the page where it matters most.
Campari at 22ml against that build pushes the drink to a dark red-brown. A Paper Plane is
a glowing orange. Anyone who arrives from search, sees a deep red drink in the photo,
makes the recipe and gets orange has been told something untrue by the picture.

Three options, in order of what I would do:

1. **Make it properly.** Aperol is around $25, and a mid-priced amaro like Montenegro or
   Ramazzotti is around $25 in place of Nonino, which is closer to $50. Call it $50 for
   the highest-traffic unphotographed page on the site. Nonino and Montenegro are close
   enough in colour that the substitution is invisible.
2. **Aperol only, skip the amaro for the shot.** Bourbon, Aperol and lemon land very close
   to the right orange, because the bourbon is already supplying the amber the amaro
   would. About $25. The colour would be honest; the glass would not hold the recipe. Your
   call whether that crosses the line, and it is a closer call than the Campari version.
3. **Shoot it last with everything else and accept it stays unphotographed for now.** The
   page ranks without a photo already.

What I would not do is shoot it with Campari and move on.

## The three to defer

**Aviation.** Gin 60, maraschino 15, crème de violette 7, lemon 22. The violette is the
colour and there is no honest way to fake pale lavender. Rev. 2 suggested food colouring;
rev. 3 withdraws that, on the same reasoning as Paper Plane. Roughly zero traffic. Defer.

**Hanky-Panky.** Gin, sweet vermouth, Fernet Branca. Fernet is around $30 for a page with
roughly no traffic. Defer, and revisit if a gin bottle enters the house for another reason.

**Bijou.** Gin, green Chartreuse, sweet vermouth. Chartreuse is expensive and frequently
out of stock. Never buy it for a photograph. Defer indefinitely.

Deferring three is fine. Eleven or twelve strong photos beats fourteen where three are
obviously staged wrong, and the publish commit can ship with the deferred pages using the
existing layout.

## Shoot every pour, starting now

Instagram is the chosen growth channel and still photos are its lowest-reach format. From
here on, every session captures a short video of the pour out of the frozen bottle before
the still frame: phone propped, one take, six to fifteen seconds, no talking. Cold batch
pours slow and viscous and the glass fogs on contact. Details in
`claude/instagram-plan-2026-08-23.md`.

## Craft notes

- Frost: a glass straight from the freezer frosts the same whatever is inside. A 50/50
  glycerin and water spritz holds droplets for minutes. Remember the 08-22 rule: frost is
  a veil, not a curtain. A deep-frozen thick glass fogs over completely and hides the
  drink.
- Clear ice: directional freezing in a lidless cooler, 24 hours ahead.
- The grade converges on staged drinks because the white balance anchor reads ice, frost
  and rim speculars, never the liquid. Keep one of those in frame and watch for the 0.35
  anchor-saturation warning on the deepest reds.
- Mix stand-ins to the final colour at final dilution, not to recipe proportions.
- Step back further than looks right. The template is 47% headroom with the base at 91%.
